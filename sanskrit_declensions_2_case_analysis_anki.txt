Rāma goes on foot from Ayodhyā to the forest with Sītā to keep the promise of his father.</big></big><br><br><small>Case of each word, and why?</small></center>	<center><big><big>हे लक्ष्मण रामः पितुः प्रतिज्ञां पालयितुं सीतया सह पद्भ्याम् अयोध्यायाः वनं गच्छति</big></big><br>he lakṣmaṇa rāmaḥ pituḥ pratijñāṃ pālayituṃ sītayā saha padbhyām ayodhyāyāḥ vanaṃ gacchati</center><br><table><tr><td>Lakṣmaṇa</td><td>लक्ष्मण</td><td><b>mVs</b></td><td>He is being spoken to (“O …!”); the vocative calls the listener and plays no part in the action.</td></tr><tr><td>Rāma</td><td>रामः</td><td><b>mNs</b></td><td>He does the going: the subject of the active verb “goes”.</td></tr><tr><td>foot</td><td>पद्भ्याम्</td><td><b>mId</b></td><td>The means he travels by: “by (his two) feet”. English “on” is misleading; it is not a place, so not locative.</td></tr><tr><td>Ayodhyā</td><td>अयोध्यायाः</td><td><b>fAbs</b></td><td>The place the movement starts from (“from”).</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वनं</td><td><b>nAs</b></td><td>Where a verb of motion ends up: “goes to the forest”. Nothing is done to the forest, so it is not a direct object.</td></tr><tr><td>Sītā</td><td>सीतया</td><td><b>fIs</b></td><td>Accompaniment: she goes along with him (सह “with”), not as a tool he uses.</td></tr><tr><td>promise</td><td>प्रतिज्ञां</td><td><b>fAs</b></td><td>The thing that gets kept: direct object of the infinitive पालयितुम् “to keep”. The purpose is the keeping, not the promise.<br>or <b>fGs</b> — if the purpose is said with a verbal noun, प्रतिज्ञायाः पालनाय “for the keeping of the promise”, the promise is the object of that “-ing” word.</td></tr><tr><td>father</td><td>पितुः</td><td><b>mGs</b></td><td>Possession: it is his father’s promise.<br>or <b>mGs</b> — प्रतिज्ञा “a promising” is itself made from a verb, and the father is the one who promised, so he is also the subject of an “-ing” word.</td></tr></table>
