case_analysis:laksmana-rama-padbhyam-vanam	<center><big><big>O Lakṣmaṇa! Rāma goes on foot from Ayodhyā to the forest with Sītā to keep the promise of his father.</big></big><br><br><small>Case of each word, and why?</small></center>	<center><big><big>हे लक्ष्मण रामः पितुः प्रतिज्ञां पालयितुं सीतया सह पद्भ्याम् अयोध्यायाः वनं गच्छति</big></big><br>he lakṣmaṇa rāmaḥ pituḥ pratijñāṃ pālayituṃ sītayā saha padbhyām ayodhyāyāḥ vanaṃ gacchati</center><br><table><tr><td>Lakṣmaṇa</td><td>लक्ष्मण</td><td><b>mVs</b></td><td>He is being spoken to (“O …!”); the vocative calls the listener and plays no part in the action.</td></tr><tr><td>Rāma</td><td>रामः</td><td><b>mNs</b></td><td>He does the going: the subject of the active verb “goes”.</td></tr><tr><td>foot</td><td>पद्भ्याम्</td><td><b>mId</b></td><td>The means he travels by: “by (his two) feet”. English “on” is misleading; it is not a place, so not locative.</td></tr><tr><td>Ayodhyā</td><td>अयोध्यायाः</td><td><b>fAbs</b></td><td>The place the movement starts from (“from”).</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वनं</td><td><b>nAs</b></td><td>Where a verb of motion ends up: “goes to the forest”. Nothing is done to the forest, so it is not a direct object.</td></tr><tr><td>Sītā</td><td>सीतया</td><td><b>fIs</b></td><td>Accompaniment: she goes along with him (सह “with”), not as a tool he uses.</td></tr><tr><td>promise</td><td>प्रतिज्ञां</td><td><b>fAs</b></td><td>The thing that gets kept: direct object of the infinitive पालयितुम् “to keep”. The purpose is the keeping, not the promise.<br>or <b>fGs</b> — if the purpose is said with a verbal noun, प्रतिज्ञायाः पालनाय “for the keeping of the promise”, the promise is the object of that “-ing” word.</td></tr><tr><td>father</td><td>पितुः</td><td><b>mGs</b></td><td>Possession: it is his father’s promise.<br>or <b>mGs</b> — प्रतिज्ञा “a promising” is itself made from a verb, and the father is the one who promised, so he is also the subject of an “-ing” word.</td></tr></table>
case_analysis:sita-aham-tvaya-saha	<center><big><big>Sītā says: O Rāma, I go to the forest with you.</big></big><br><br><small>Case of each word, and why?</small></center>	<center><big><big>सीता वदति हे राम अहं त्वया सह वनं गच्छामि</big></big><br>sītā vadati he rāma ahaṃ tvayā saha vanaṃ gacchāmi</center><br><table><tr><td>Sītā</td><td>सीता</td><td><b>fNs</b></td><td>She does the saying: subject of the active verb “says”.</td></tr><tr><td>Rāma</td><td>राम</td><td><b>mVs</b></td><td>Sītā is speaking to him (“O Rāma”).</td></tr><tr><td>I</td><td>अहं</td><td><b>Ns</b></td><td>The one who goes: subject of “go” (गच्छामि, first person to match).</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वनं</td><td><b>nAs</b></td><td>The destination of the verb of motion “go”.</td></tr><tr><td>you</td><td>त्वया</td><td><b>Is</b></td><td>Accompaniment: “with you”, marked by सह.</td></tr></table>
case_analysis:rama-laksmana-api-vanam	<center><big><big>Rāma goes to the forest, and Lakṣmaṇa too goes to the forest.</big></big><br><br><small>Case of each word, and why?</small></center>	<center><big><big>रामः वनं गच्छति लक्ष्मणः अपि वनं गच्छति</big></big><br>rāmaḥ vanaṃ gacchati lakṣmaṇaḥ api vanaṃ gacchati</center><br><table><tr><td>Rāma</td><td>रामः</td><td><b>mNs</b></td><td>Subject of the first “goes”.</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वनं</td><td><b>nAs</b></td><td>Destination of a verb of motion.</td></tr><tr><td>Lakṣmaṇa</td><td>लक्ष्मणः</td><td><b>mNs</b></td><td>Subject of the second “goes”; अपि “too” is indeclinable and changes nothing.</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वनं</td><td><b>nAs</b></td><td>Destination again, the same as for Rāma.</td></tr></table>
case_analysis:rama-caturdasa-varsani	<center><big><big>Rāma lives in the forest for fourteen years.</big></big><br><br><small>Case of each word, and why?</small></center>	<center><big><big>रामः चतुर्दश वर्षाणि वने वसति</big></big><br>rāmaḥ caturdaśa varṣāṇi vane vasati</center><br><table><tr><td>Rāma</td><td>रामः</td><td><b>mNs</b></td><td>He does the living: subject of the active verb “lives”.</td></tr><tr><td>forest</td><td>वने</td><td><b>nLs</b></td><td>The place where the action happens (“in”).</td></tr><tr><td>fourteen</td><td>चतुर्दश</td><td><b>Ap</b></td><td>(beyond the handout) A number above four works like an adjective with no gender of its own: it agrees with वर्षाणि.</td></tr><tr><td>years</td><td>वर्षाणि</td><td><b>nAp</b></td><td>(beyond the handout) How long the action lasts. Sanskrit treats a stretch of time as the ground an action covers, the way the accusative marks the ground a verb of motion covers, so “for fourteen years” takes the accusative with no preposition.</td></tr></table>
