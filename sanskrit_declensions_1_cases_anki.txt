case_name:english:nominative	<center><big><big><big><big>Nominative</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>प्रथमा</big></big></big></big><br><big>prathamā</big> “first”<br><br>case 1<br>naming: the subject<br>cue: subject<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>masc. / fem.</small></td><td><b>◌ः</b></td><td><b>औ</b></td><td><b>अः</b></td></tr><tr><td><small>neuter</small></td><td><b>—</b></td><td><b>ई</b></td><td><b>इ</b></td></tr></table><small>Pāṇini’s ending is स् (s); the handout writes it as visarga ः.</small></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>1a</b> Subject of an active verb</li><li><b>1b</b> Subjective complement: a noun linked to the subject by “be” or “become”; both words take the same case</li><li><b>1c</b> A noun in apposition to the subject (a second noun naming the same person)</li><li><b>1d</b> An adjective modifying a noun in the nominative (adjectives take the case of their noun)</li><li><b>1e</b> The neuter nominative singular can work as an indeclinable adverb: सुखम् “happily”</li></ul>
case_name:english:accusative	<center><big><big><big><big>Accusative</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>द्वितीया</big></big></big></big><br><big>dvitīyā</big> “second”<br><br>case 2<br>the direct object; the destination of motion<br>cue: object; “to” a place<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>masc. / fem.</small></td><td><b>अम्</b></td><td><b>औ</b></td><td><b>अः</b></td></tr><tr><td><small>neuter</small></td><td><b>—</b></td><td><b>ई</b></td><td><b>इ</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>2a</b> Direct object of a transitive verb (the thing acted upon)</li><li><b>2b</b> Objective complement: a noun linked to the direct object by an implied “be” or “become”</li><li><b>2c</b> Destination of a verb of motion</li><li><b>2d</b> Occasionally, with some prepositions (above, below, near, around, towards…)</li></ul>
case_name:english:instrumental	<center><big><big><big><big>Instrumental</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>तृतीया</big></big></big></big><br><big>tṛtīyā</big> “third”<br><br>case 3<br>the agent or instrument<br>cue: “by”, “with”<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>all genders</small></td><td><b>आ</b></td><td><b>भ्याम्</b></td><td><b>भिः</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>3a</b> The instrument used in the action</li><li><b>3b</b> The agent (doer) of a passive verb or passive participle</li><li><b>3c</b> Accompaniment, usually with सह, समम् or साकम् (“with”), which can also be left out</li><li><b>3d</b> विना (“without”) usually takes the instrumental (sometimes the dative, 4f)</li><li><b>3e</b> Cause or reason (हेतु): “because of, owing to” (the ablative can also express cause, 5c)</li><li><b>3f</b> With सदृश and तुल्य (“equal to, same as”)</li><li><b>3g</b> An instrumental noun can be used adverbially: सुखेन “happily”</li><li><b>3h</b> In expressions like “by name”, “by nature”</li><li><b>3i</b> With अलम् and कृतम् (“enough!”): अलं दुःखेन “enough with sorrow”</li><li><b>3j</b> किम् + a noun in the instrumental = “what is the use of …?”</li></ul>
case_name:english:dative	<center><big><big><big><big>Dative</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>चतुर्थी</big></big></big></big><br><big>caturthī</big> “fourth”<br><br>case 4<br>the indirect object<br>cue: “for”, “for the sake of”, “unto”<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>all genders</small></td><td><b>ए</b></td><td><b>भ्याम्</b></td><td><b>भ्यः</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>4a</b> Indirect object of verbs like “give, send, show”: the one to or for whom the action is done</li><li><b>4b</b> The person or thing for whose advantage the action is done (“for the sake of”)</li><li><b>4c</b> The purpose of an action</li><li><b>4d</b> The object of verbs showing malice or anger</li><li><b>4e</b> With the particles of reverence नमः and स्वस्ति</li><li><b>4f</b> विना (“without”) sometimes takes the dative (usually the instrumental, 3d)</li></ul>
case_name:english:ablative	<center><big><big><big><big>Ablative</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>पञ्चमी</big></big></big></big><br><big>pañcamī</big> “fifth”<br><br>case 5<br>origin, source<br>cue: “from”, “out of”, “due to”<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>all genders</small></td><td><b>अः</b></td><td><b>भ्याम्</b></td><td><b>भ्यः</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>5a</b> The place or time from which the action originates</li><li><b>5b</b> The object of verbs meaning “to desist from”, “to protect (from)”, “to fear”</li><li><b>5c</b> Cause or motive, “out of” (similar to the instrumental, 3e)</li><li><b>5d</b> With a comparative: the thing compared against (“other than”, “higher than”)</li><li><b>5e</b> With some prepositions (before, east of, after, until…)</li></ul>
case_name:english:genitive	<center><big><big><big><big>Genitive</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>षष्ठी</big></big></big></big><br><big>ṣaṣṭhī</big> “sixth”<br><br>case 6<br>possession: a relation between two nouns, not to the verb<br>cue: “of”, “'s”, “for”<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>all genders</small></td><td><b>अः</b></td><td><b>ओः</b></td><td><b>आम्</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>6a</b> Possession: the possessor is placed in the genitive, usually before the thing possessed</li><li><b>6b</b> Replaces the English verb “to have”: “Rāma has a wife” = “of Rāma there is a wife”</li><li><b>6c</b> Sometimes used in place of the dative with verbs like “give”, “show”</li><li><b>6d</b> Often used in place of the dative to give the sense “for” (e.g. “qualified for”)</li><li><b>6e</b> The subject or object of an “-ing word” (a noun derived from a verb)</li></ul>
case_name:english:locative	<center><big><big><big><big>Locative</big></big></big></big><br><small>Sanskrit name?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><big>सप्तमी</big></big></big></big><br><big>saptamī</big> “seventh”<br><br>case 7<br>location in space or time<br>cue: “in”, “on”, “at”<br><br><b>Endings</b><table><tr><td></td><td><small>singular</small></td><td><small>dual</small></td><td><small>plural</small></td></tr><tr><td><small>all genders</small></td><td><b>इ</b></td><td><b>ओः</b></td><td><b>सु</b></td></tr></table></center><br><b>Uses</b><ul><li><b>7a</b> The place or time where the action takes place (in, at, on, upon, among)</li><li><b>7b</b> “With reference to”, “with regard to”, in terms of subject matter</li><li><b>7c</b> The object of love or devotion</li><li><b>7d</b> The absolute construction (सति सप्तमी), showing a simultaneous action</li></ul>
