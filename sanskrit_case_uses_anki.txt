case_use:1a	<center><big>The subject (the doer) of an active verb</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Nominative (प्रथमा)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>1a</b> Subject of an active verb<br><br><big><b>गुरुः</b> वदति</big><br>The <u>teacher</u> speaks.<br><br><big><b>जनकः</b> नयति</big><br><u>Janaka</u> leads.</center>
case_use:1b	<center><big>A noun linked to the subject by “be” or “become” (“Rāma is king”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Nominative (प्रथमा)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>1b</b> Subjective complement: a noun linked to the subject by “be” or “become”; both words take the same case<br><br><big>रामः <b>नृपः</b> अस्ति</big><br>Rāma is <u>king</u>.<br><br><big>पुत्राः <b>वीराः</b> भवन्ति</big><br>The sons become <u>heroes</u>.</center>
case_use:1c	<center><big>A noun in apposition to the subject (“Rāma, the hero, conquers”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Nominative (प्रथमा)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>1c</b> A noun in apposition to the subject (a second noun naming the same person)<br><br><big>रामः <b>वीरः</b> जयति</big><br>Rāma, the <u>hero</u>, conquers.<br><br><big>नृपः <b>वीरः</b> आगच्छति</big><br>The king, the <u>hero</u>, comes.</center>
case_use:1d	<center><big>An adjective describing the subject (“the beautiful boy comes”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Nominative (प्रथमा)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>1d</b> An adjective modifying a noun in the nominative (adjectives take the case of their noun)<br><br><big><b>सुन्दरः</b> बालः आगच्छति</big><br>The <u>beautiful</u> boy comes.<br><br><big>मृगः <b>हतः</b> न धावति</big><br>The <u>dead</u> deer does not run.</center>
case_use:1e	<center><big>Which case, in the neuter singular, can work as an indeclinable adverb (सुखम् “happily”)?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Nominative (प्रथमा)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>1e</b> The neuter nominative singular can work as an indeclinable adverb: सुखम् “happily”<br><br><big>सः अतीव धार्मिकः भूमिपः <b>सुखं</b> जीवति</big><br>That very righteous king lives <u>happily</u>.</center>
case_use:2a	<center><big>The direct object of a transitive verb (the thing acted upon)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Accusative (द्वितीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>2a</b> Direct object of a transitive verb (the thing acted upon)<br><br><big><b>रामं</b> बोधामः</big><br>We know <u>Rāma</u>.<br><br><big>नृपः <b>देशं</b> जयति</big><br>The king conquers the <u>country</u>.</center>
case_use:2b	<center><big>A noun linked to the direct object by an implied “be” (“we know Rāma to be a hero”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Accusative (द्वितीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>2b</b> Objective complement: a noun linked to the direct object by an implied “be” or “become”<br><br><big>रामं <b>वीरं</b> बोधामः</big><br>We know Rāma to be a <u>hero</u>.<br><br><big>रामः कपोतं <b>काकं</b> पश्यति</big><br>Rāma sees the pigeon as a <u>crow</u>.</center>
case_use:2c	<center><big>The destination of a verb of motion (“goes to the forest”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Accusative (द्वितीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>2c</b> Destination of a verb of motion<br><br><big>रामः <b>वनं</b> गच्छति</big><br>Rāma goes to the <u>forest</u>.<br><br><big>दासः <b>कूपं</b> गच्छति</big><br>The servant goes to the <u>well</u>.</center>
case_use:2d	<center><big>Which case do some prepositions meaning “above, below, near, around” occasionally take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Accusative (द्वितीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>2d</b> Occasionally, with some prepositions (above, below, near, around, towards…)<br><br><big><b>दशरथं</b> प्रति वदति</big><br>He speaks to <u>Daśaratha</u>.</center>
case_use:3a	<center><big>The instrument used in an action (“kills with an arrow”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3a</b> The instrument used in the action<br><br><big>सः <b>शरेण</b> हन्ति</big><br>He kills with an <u>arrow</u>.<br><br><big>बालकः अपि सः वीरः बहून् राक्षसान् <b>इषुभिः</b> हन्ति</big><br>Though only a boy, that hero kills many rākṣasas with his <u>arrows</u>.</center>
case_use:3b	<center><big>The agent (doer) of a passive verb or passive participle (“killed by the king”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3b</b> The agent (doer) of a passive verb or passive participle<br><br><big>सः <b>नृपेण</b> हतः</big><br>He was killed by the <u>king</u>.<br><br><big><b>रामेण</b> अभिहता निशाचरचमूः</big><br>The army of night-stalkers was struck down by <u>Rāma</u>.</center>
case_use:3c	<center><big>Accompaniment, “with”, usually alongside सह, समम् or साकम्</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3c</b> Accompaniment, usually with सह, समम् or साकम् (“with”), which can also be left out<br><br><big>रामः <b>सीतया</b> सह गच्छति</big><br>Rāma goes with <u>Sītā</u>.<br><br><big>रामः <b>मुनिना</b> सह अरण्यं गच्छति</big><br>Rāma goes to the forest with the <u>sage</u>.</center>
case_use:3d	<center><big>Which case does विना (“without”) usually take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3d</b> विना (“without”) usually takes the instrumental (sometimes the dative, 4f)<br><br><big>रामः <b>सीतया</b> विना वनं गच्छति</big><br>Rāma goes to the forest without <u>Sītā</u>.<br><br><big><b>पुत्रेण</b> विना न जीवामि</big><br>Without my <u>son</u> I cannot live.</center>
case_use:3e	<center><big>Cause or reason (हेतु): “by, owing to” (“the jīva is bound by sorrow”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3e</b> Cause or reason (हेतु): “because of, owing to” (the ablative can also express cause, 5c)<br><br><big>जीवः <b>दुःखेन</b> बद्धः</big><br>The jīva is bound by (owing to) <u>sorrow</u>.<br><br><big>तव <b>दुष्कृतेन</b> अहं दुःखम् अनुभवामि</big><br>Because of your <u>misdeed</u> I suffer sorrow.</center>
case_use:3f	<center><big>Which case do सदृश and तुल्य (“equal to, same as”) take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3f</b> With सदृश and तुल्य (“equal to, same as”)</center>
case_use:3g	<center><big>Besides the neuter nominative (सुखम्), which case of a noun can serve as an adverb (“happily”)?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3g</b> An instrumental noun can be used adverbially: सुखेन “happily”</center>
case_use:3h	<center><big>Expressions like “by name” or “by nature”</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3h</b> In expressions like “by name”, “by nature”</center>
case_use:3i	<center><big>Which case do अलम् and कृतम् (“enough with…!”) take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3i</b> With अलम् and कृतम् (“enough!”): अलं दुःखेन “enough with sorrow”<br><br><big>अलं <b>दुःखेन</b></big><br>Enough with <u>sorrow</u>!</center>
case_use:3j	<center><big>किम् + which case of a noun means “what is the use of …?”</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Instrumental (तृतीया)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>3j</b> किम् + a noun in the instrumental = “what is the use of …?”<br><br><big>किं <b>धनेन</b></big><br>What is the use of <u>wealth</u>?</center>
case_use:4a	<center><big>The indirect object of verbs like “give, send, show” (“gives the books to the students”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4a</b> Indirect object of verbs like “give, send, show”: the one to or for whom the action is done<br><br><big>गुरुः <b>शिष्येभ्यः</b> पुस्तकानि यच्छति</big><br>The teacher gives the books to the <u>students</u>.</center>
case_use:4b	<center><big>The person or thing for whose benefit the action is done (“rules for the sake of the people”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4b</b> The person or thing for whose advantage the action is done (“for the sake of”)<br><br><big>राजा <b>जनेभ्यः</b> राज्यं करोति</big><br>The king rules for (the sake of) the <u>people</u>.</center>
case_use:4c	<center><big>The purpose of an action (“goes for war”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4c</b> The purpose of an action<br><br><big><b>युद्धाय</b> गच्छति</big><br>He goes for <u>war</u> (with the purpose of fighting).<br><br><big>ब्राह्मणैः सह सः <b>प्रजायै</b> यज्ञं करोति</big><br>With the brahmins he performs a sacrifice for <u>offspring</u>.</center>
case_use:4d	<center><big>The object of verbs of anger or malice (“angry with the son”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4d</b> The object of verbs showing malice or anger<br><br><big>पिता <b>पुत्राय</b> कुप्यति</big><br>The father is angry with the <u>son</u>.</center>
case_use:4e	<center><big>Which case do the particles of reverence नमः and स्वस्ति take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4e</b> With the particles of reverence नमः and स्वस्ति<br><br><big><b>ईशाय</b> नमः</big><br>Salutations to <u>Īśvara</u>.<br><br><big><b>रामाय</b> तस्मै नमः</big><br>Salutations to that <u>Rāma</u>.</center>
case_use:4f	<center><big>Besides its usual case, which case does विना (“without”) sometimes take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Dative (चतुर्थी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>4f</b> विना (“without”) sometimes takes the dative (usually the instrumental, 3d)</center>
case_use:5a	<center><big>The place or time an action starts from (“goes from the forest”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Ablative (पञ्चमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>5a</b> The place or time from which the action originates<br><br><big>मुनिः <b>वनात्</b> गच्छति</big><br>The sage goes from the <u>forest</u>.</center>
case_use:5b	<center><big>The object of verbs meaning “desist from”, “protect from” or “fear”</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Ablative (पञ्चमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>5b</b> The object of verbs meaning “to desist from”, “to protect (from)”, “to fear”<br><br><big>ईश्वरः नरान् <b>दुःखात्</b> रक्षति</big><br>God protects men from <u>misery</u>.<br><br><big><b>राक्षसेभ्यः</b> ऋषयः अपि उद्विजन्ते</big><br>Even the seers shrink in fear from the <u>rākṣasas</u>.</center>
case_use:5c	<center><big>Cause or motive: “out of” (“out of anger he strikes the boy”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Ablative (पञ्चमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>5c</b> Cause or motive, “out of” (similar to the instrumental, 3e)<br><br><big><b>क्रोधात्</b> बालं तुदति</big><br>Out of <u>anger</u> he strikes the boy.<br><br><big>त्वम् अपि अहम् इव <b>पुत्रात्</b> शोकम् अनुभवसि</big><br>You too, like me, will suffer grief on account of a <u>son</u>.</center>
case_use:5d	<center><big>The thing compared against, with a comparative (“other than”, “higher than”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Ablative (पञ्चमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>5d</b> With a comparative: the thing compared against (“other than”, “higher than”)<br><br><big><b>आत्मनः</b> अन्यः</big><br>Other than <u>ātmā</u>.<br><br><big><b>रामात्</b> न अस्ति परायणं परतरम्</big><br>There is no higher refuge than <u>Rāma</u>.</center>
case_use:5e	<center><big>Which case do prepositions meaning “before, east of, after, until” take?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Ablative (पञ्चमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>5e</b> With some prepositions (before, east of, after, until…)</center>
case_use:6a	<center><big>The possessor (“Rāma's book”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Genitive (षष्ठी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>6a</b> Possession: the possessor is placed in the genitive, usually before the thing possessed<br><br><big><b>रामस्य</b> पुस्तकम्</big><br><u>Rāma's</u> book (the book of Rāma).<br><br><big><b>रामस्य</b> दासः अस्मि अहम्</big><br>I am a servant of <u>Rāma</u>.</center>
case_use:6b	<center><big>Sanskrit has no verb “to have”: “Rāma has a wife” puts Rāma in which case?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Genitive (षष्ठी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>6b</b> Replaces the English verb “to have”: “Rāma has a wife” = “of Rāma there is a wife”<br><br><big><b>रामस्य</b> भार्या अस्ति</big><br><u>Rāma</u> has a wife (of Rāma there is a wife).<br><br><big><b>नृपस्य</b> दुःखं न अस्ति</big><br>The <u>king</u> has no sorrow (of the king there is no sorrow).</center>
case_use:6c	<center><big>Which case sometimes replaces the dative with verbs like “give” or “show”?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Genitive (षष्ठी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>6c</b> Sometimes used in place of the dative with verbs like “give”, “show”</center>
case_use:6d	<center><big>Which case often replaces the dative for the sense “for” (“qualified for”)?</big></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Genitive (षष्ठी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>6d</b> Often used in place of the dative to give the sense “for” (e.g. “qualified for”)</center>
case_use:6e	<center><big>The subject or object of an “-ing word”, a noun derived from a verb (“his grasping of the rope”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Genitive (षष्ठी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>6e</b> The subject or object of an “-ing word” (a noun derived from a verb)<br><br><big>तस्य <b>रज्जोः</b> ग्रहणम् रक्षति तम्</big><br>His grasping of the <u>rope</u> saved him.<br><br><big>त्वम् एव <b>प्रजानां</b> रक्षकः असि</big><br>You alone are the protector of the <u>people</u>.</center>
case_use:7a	<center><big>The place or time where an action takes place (“lives in the forest”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Locative (सप्तमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>7a</b> The place or time where the action takes place (in, at, on, upon, among)<br><br><big><b>वने</b> वसति</big><br>He lives in the <u>forest</u>.<br><br><big>तस्मिन् <b>वने</b> ब्राह्मणस्य पुत्रः वसति</big><br>In that <u>forest</u> lives a brahmin's son.</center>
case_use:7b	<center><big>“With reference to, with regard to” a subject matter</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Locative (सप्तमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>7b</b> “With reference to”, “with regard to”, in terms of subject matter<br><br><big>तेषां <b>हनने</b> कः समर्थः</big><br>Who is capable of (with regard to) <u>killing</u> them?</center>
case_use:7c	<center><big>The object of love or devotion (“the king loves Rāma”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Locative (सप्तमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>7c</b> The object of love or devotion<br><br><big>राजा <b>रामे</b> स्निह्यति</big><br>The king loves <u>Rāma</u>.<br><br><big>तस्मात् नृपः <b>रामे</b> स्निह्यति विशेषतः</big><br>So the king loves <u>Rāma</u> above all.</center>
case_use:7d	<center><big>The “absolute” construction (सति …), showing an action happening at the same time as the main one</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Locative (सप्तमी)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>7d</b> The absolute construction (सति सप्तमी), showing a simultaneous action</center>
case_use:8a	<center><big>Directly addressing someone (“O Rāma!”)</big><br><small>Which case?</small></center>	<center><big><big><big><b>Vocative (संबोधनम्)</b></big></big></big><br><br><b>8a</b> Direct address<br><br><big>हे <b>राम</b></big><br>O <u>Rāma</u>!<br><br><big>हे <b>मूर्ख</b></big><br>O <u>fool</u>!</center>
